# Keep the Crew, Mine the Neighbors

No existing project already does most of what Claude Crew needs, so the right call is to **continue as designed and borrow specific components**, not to fork, rebase or adopt. About 80 Factorio LLM-agent repos and 20 portal mods appeared between June and October 2026, almost all with 0 to 3 stars ([broad sweep, GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=factorio+agent&type=repositories)). Three of them independently converged on Claude Crew's architecture within the last month: a characterless planner plus character workers ([llm-play-factorio](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio)), a strategist plus a fast low-effort executor ([factorio-codex](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex)), and "scripted loops do the work; the model is the fallback" ([factorio-crewmate](https://github.com/quartermeat/factorio-crewmate)). That convergence validates the design, and it also means the architecture alone is no longer novel. What is still unclaimed is the combination: several legit-rules LLM characters in the same multiplayer game as humans, commanded from plain chat, run by a coordinator writing machine-checkable work orders. The closest projects each solve a different half and rule out the other half on purpose. llm-play-factorio destroys a joining human's character, factorio-codex makes chat control and multiple bodies explicit non-goals, and the closest multiplayer crew, [watdafawx/ai-crew](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew), teleports and pulls items remotely. The real risk of building from scratch sits on the game side. llm-play-factorio's vendored, MIT-licensed factorio-mcp mod has about 13.4k Lua lines and 71 tools hardened over four runs, against Claude Crew's ~3.2k. That is the layer to mine hardest. For tonight, the useful borrowing is almost all prompt text and pre-flight checks, because any Lua change has to reach the friend's client before they join.

## Eighty young repos, and none covers the core use case

The field is crowded but shallow. The most-downloaded LLM mod on the portal has 1,446 downloads and was abandoned with "Multiplayer does not work and I never have plans to make it work" ([ai-player](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/ai-player)). The scripted, non-LLM Companion Drones has 76,554 ([Companion Drones](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/Companion_Drones)), so players clearly want helpers that build their ghosts and follow them around. The frontier moved this autumn: a community GPT-6 Astra harness launched rockets in vanilla and Space Age ([HN 49608875](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49608875); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/gpt-6-astra-pokemon-champion-in-18-hours-potato-farmer-after-one-creeper-mishap/)), and Anthropic's June launch post says Claude Fable 5 "autonomously plays Factorio" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5)). "Can an LLM launch a rocket" is therefore settled. What remains open is verifiable legit play, cost, and cooperation with humans. The r/factorio reaction to Vals AI's unpublished Space Age claim was "Were blueprints allowed?" ([Traictory](https://traictory.com/news/2026-09-19-astra-factorio-space-age)). The only cost figure anywhere is second-hand: about $4,500 for the Astra vanilla run ([conveyer LEARNINGS](https://github.com/nulljosh/conveyer/blob/HEAD/docs/LEARNINGS.md)). Against that, Claude Crew's measured +6 techs for $35–46 from a mid-game save is a datum nobody else publishes.

## Three neighbors each solve a different half

### llm-play-factorio and factorio-mcp: the best game layer, hostile to humans

This is the most directly comparable project. A characterless Opus planner coordinates up to 16 character-owning workers (≤6 in practice). Its base-game rockets came at 13:33:01, 10:25:25 and 8:26:02 game time, and a Space Age run reached Vulcanus, Fulgora and Gleba with 145 launches before pausing ([README](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio); [lessons-run4](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/docs/lessons-run4.md)). Its strength is on the game side. The vendored factorio-mcp walks with the engine pathfinder and re-asserts `walking_state` every tick, mines via the engine at vanilla speed ("measured live: 0.496 iron ore/s"), crafts through the real queue, walks into reach before every map action, and contains **no `teleport` anywhere** ([walk.lua](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/mod/factorio-mcp/scripts/actions/walk.lua); [mine.lua](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/mod/factorio-mcp/scripts/actions/mine.lua)). It also adds fog of war, a blueprint-provenance gate, and an RCON client that keeps one command in flight with idempotent `enqueue` by `request_id` ([UPSTREAM.md](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/UPSTREAM.md)). Its lessons files are the richest operational knowledge in the field. "Size jobs to one stint… Give each job one target and the measurement that shows it is done" came after about 30 jobs died at the token limit ([lessons-run4](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/docs/lessons-run4.md)), which is direct field evidence for Claude Crew's `done_when` design.

Its weaknesses are exactly Claude Crew's strengths. There is **no orchestrator code**: the planner is an interactive Claude Code session, assignments are prose in a 3,546-line `board.md`, and verification is the planner's read-only RCON scripts plus worker self-reports ([board.md](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/docs/run4/board.md)). Every worker step is a Sonnet call. The fork is built against human co-play: a default-on setting puts joining players in spectator mode and **destroys their character, items included** ([spectators.lua](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/mod/factorio-mcp/scripts/spectators.lua)). It publishes no cost data, the repo root has no license, and it is four days old with a bus factor of one.

The borrowable code is in the MIT mod: `scripts/actions/walk.lua` (pathfinder-answer handling, stuck and deadline rules), `approach.lua` (shared reach helper with step-aside), `placement.lua` (names the blocker, missing ore, water), `belts.lua` (`trace_belt`/`measure_belt`, per-lane contents and fill), `alerts.lua` (an `ALERTS:` delta on every result), `rpc_log.lua` + `archive.lua` + `rocket.lua` (replay log, 10-minute save archive, `run-result.json`), `follow.lua` (`/follow-cams` with status lines for watching humans), and `space.lua` with the tested engine facts in [space-tooling.md](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/docs/space-tooling.md). `scripts/route/` is GPL and must not be copied. `lpf/`, `scripts/` and the docs are unlicensed and can be read for ideas only.

### factorio-codex: the deepest deterministic chores, one body by design

Codex is the most mature engineering in the field. It has 617 commits, about 116 test files with a 2.0.77 API fixture, MIT, and it targets exactly Claude Crew's Factorio build ([repo](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex); [AGENT-PLAY-PERFORMANCE.md](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/docs/AGENT-PLAY-PERFORMANCE.md)). Its strategist/pilot split is Claude Crew's coordinator/actor split. The strategist (`gpt-6.1-sol`, medium) gets a mechanically read-only MCP surface with build tools forced to dry-run, and it writes a strict-schema ledger (≤2 packages per update, 8 KB cap, cycle rejection). The pilot (`gpt-6-luna`, low, fast) is the sole gameplay writer ([ledger.ts](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/companion/src/coordination/ledger.ts); [README](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/README.md)). The mod does more on its own than any other project. It groups machines into lines with states (`starved/output_full/no_power/frozen…`) and `rate_per_min/utilisation`, auto-supplies (fetch → smelt → hand-craft four levels → hand-gather) with `SUPPLY_SHORTFALL` reasons, pre-fetches a layout's whole bill or places nothing (`LAYOUT_CHECK_FAILED`), keeps a per-(action, target) repeat counter, runs upkeep, and spreads heavy reads across ticks under an ~8 ms budget ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/AGENTS.md); [tasks.lua](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/mod/agentic-companion/scripts/tasks.lua)). Its Space Age tools move the body by rocket, platform and pod.

The limits are deliberate. Codex lists "multiple controllable bodies or gameplay writers… game-chat control… built-in model loops" as non-goals, forces every other client into spectator mode with their character destroyed, and requires a real game client per body ([AGENTS.md](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/AGENTS.md); [companion.lua](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/mod/agentic-companion/scripts/companion.lua)). Its best recorded early run reached about 49 iron plates and Electronics in 20 minutes, and no live rocket is evidenced ([AGENT-PLAY-PERFORMANCE.md](https://github.com/LukaVodopivec/factorio-codex/blob/90de437/docs/AGENT-PLAY-PERFORMANCE.md)). It calls into the game through `/silent-command remote.call`, which disables achievements. Its `verify` metrics are measured after the fact and never trigger a fix. Claude Crew's `done_when` checks are part of the order itself, which is a different design.

Borrow, with attribution to Matteo Mekhail and LukaVodopivec: `scripts/factory_status.lua` + `autonomy.lua` (line states as a backend for `working`/`rate` checks), `scripts/actions/supply.lua` (procurement with reasons), `build_layout`'s all-or-nothing bill pre-fetch, the `storage.repeats` counter in `tasks.lua`, `watches.lua` (rate watches that fire once and re-arm), the 2-game-minute settle before measuring a rate, the dry-run diagnostics (`on_ore`, `mixed_ore`, `open_fluid_ports`, `belt_joins`), and `platforms.lua`/`actions/travel.lua`/`actions/rocket.lua` for Space Age. Its native `mining_state` and linked-input takeover detection assume a connected player and need adapting. Claude Crew already drives `mining_state` on playerless characters with a progress watchdog (`mod/claude-crew/scripts/jobs.lua` L223–232), which agrees with factorio-partner's 2.0.77 finding ([ENGINE-NOTES.md](https://github.com/Fatoom333/factorio-partner/blob/main/docs/ENGINE-NOTES.md)). That resolves crewmate's contrary claim, at least on this build.

### watdafawx/ai-crew: the same product idea, built on cheats

ai-crew is the closest conceptual neighbour: named helper characters that build ghosts, mine, craft, fight and pursue goals, commanded from chat, owner-scoped in multiplayer, with about 40 headless test scenarios including a 2,000-chest, 10,000-belt perf base ([README](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/README.md)). Its UX details are better than Claude Crew's today. `find_hint` flags "output full" only when nothing takes the output (`output_taken`), names the specific missing ingredient per starved recipe (`missing_input`), and rate-limits proactive hints to one per 5 minutes with no repeat for 30. Its offers are stock-aware, and declined offers cool down for 30 minutes. Its chat addressing drops unaddressed lines once more than one player is connected ([roam.lua](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/roam.lua); [orders.lua](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/orders.lua)). It also has "keep out" zones and "remember…" notes that go into every model call.

Underneath, it breaks Claude Crew's rules. `take()` inserts items from chests near the owner, the owner's pockets, belts and the logistic network **before** any walk. Movement teleports through blockages and flies jetpacks without the equipment. The body is a custom 0.2-tile, belt-immune prototype ([work.lua](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/work.lua); [move.lua](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/move.lua); [data-final-fixes.lua](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/data-final-fixes.lua)). Its LLM is a chat and NLU layer over a handful of job kinds. In multiplayer it needs the author's Windows-only DLL loader on every peer ([FSE](https://github.com/watdafawx/fse/blob/main/README.md)), and it has no coordinator, no late-game tools and no Space Age logic. Borrow `output_taken()` and `missing_input()` (MIT), the hint rate limits, and keep-out notes as coordinator constraints. Do not borrow its sourcing or movement.

### Single-purpose quarries worth naming

Four other projects each do one thing better than anyone. **factorio-partner** has a verified achievement-safe transport, owner "words" (stop/come/status/continue) parsed in the mod in the same tick, the entity under the owner's cursor attached to their chat (`selected`, for "fix *this*"), a full status-to-cause taxonomy, and shift restarts with redacted handover notes ([owner_words.lua](https://github.com/Fatoom333/factorio-partner/blob/main/mod/factorio-partner/scripts/core/owner_words.lua); [status.lua](https://github.com/Fatoom333/factorio-partner/blob/main/mod/factorio-partner/scripts/perception/status.lua)). **AI Agent Bridge** has the best transport discipline: a `calls` batch op that cut five ~105 ms RCON trips to one, reply caps that refuse an oversize reply whole instead of truncating it, bounded entity walks that report their own coverage, a storage ring with a cursor so a restarted agent "re-answers nothing", an RPC command that refuses players, and per-player cooldowns plus a daily USD cap ([ADR 0011](https://github.com/bits-orio/ai-agent-bridge/blob/main/docs/adr/0011-calls-batch-op.md); [SECURITY.md](https://github.com/bits-orio/ai-agent-bridge/blob/main/SECURITY.md)). **claude-for-factorio** adds capacity math Claude Crew's `diagnose` lacks: theoretical output with modules, beacons and productivity, a measured rate from `products_finished` deltas, and summing across qualities ([scan.lua](https://github.com/Solevaral/claude-for-factorio/blob/main/claude-for-factorio_0.1.0/scan.lua)). **Alina** has the strongest human-coexistence model: human-activity cells that expire after 30 s and block agent work within 20 tiles, owner-only protected areas, a transaction log so rollback never touches human edits, and a release gate of host plus two clients with no desync report ([conflict/manager.lua](https://github.com/KORTY-DEV/alina-ai-teammate/blob/99e72d4/factorio-mod/alina-ai-teammate_0.1.0/scripts/conflict/manager.lua)). Alina's license forbids derivative works, so take the ideas only.

## Forking would trade a novel orchestrator for a better game layer

The case for rebasing deserves a fair hearing, because the user suspects a bias toward building from scratch. Claude Crew's mod is about 3.2k Lua lines and its orchestrator about 4.7k Python lines (counted in the local repo on 2026-10-18). The MIT factorio-mcp in llm-play-factorio is about 13.4k Lua lines with ~6k test lines, runs the same 2.0.77 build, uses the same skeleton (RCON remote interface, per-character job chains), exposes a Python `Game`/`Bridge` API usable without MCP, and has launched rockets in four runs including Space Age ([factorio-mcp README](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/README.md)). If the question were only "whose character API is more battle-tested", the answer is factorio-mcp's, and Claude Crew is duplicating effort there.

Rebasing still loses. The source-level estimate for moving Claude Crew onto factorio-mcp is **two to four weeks**. That covers removing the spectator lock and auditing for playerless assumptions, re-implementing job-queue semantics, `done_when` and coordinator-only reads as new RPCs, porting late-game tools (circuit and module tools are explicit non-goals there), extracting the GPL router before handing the mod to friends, and re-running every benchmark stage. This is an estimate from reading the source, not a measurement, and the circuit/module gap is the author's own stated limitation ([factorio-mcp README](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/README.md)). The upstream is four days old with one author and calls itself "a personal research project and is not distributed". The orchestration layer, which is what Claude Crew is actually about, has nothing to rebase onto: llm-play-factorio's is Claude Code plus markdown, Codex's is one body, and ai-crew's is goal parsing. Contributing upstream is also a poor fit. Codex rejects multi-body and chat control by charter, the llm-play-factorio fork is unlicensed, linsyking has been quiet since late September, and ai-crew's cheats are a philosophy, not a bug. The honest verdict: **keep the orchestrator, and treat factorio-mcp and Codex as reference implementations for the game layer, porting module by module.** If Claude Crew's walking, mining or placement keeps producing bugs these projects have already fixed, revisit a game-layer swap then. Don't do it preemptively.

What Claude Crew does that no project does, stated honestly:

- **Several legit-rules LLM characters acting in the same multiplayer game as humans**, commanded from plain chat with `@name`/`@all`/bare names. Every project that has multiple legit bodies locks humans out, and every project that welcomes humans either cheats, is 2.1-only, or serializes one model thread.
- **A characterless coordinator writing structured work orders whose completion is checked in-game as part of the order.** Codex measures after the fact, llm-play-factorio uses prose, and crewmate has guards but no coordinator. **Caveat: this is untested end to end** (commit `0e284cc`: "Actor/scheduler/coordinator not yet tested end to end").
- **A deterministic scheduler across multiple actors that calls small models only on exceptions.** Codex's pilot loops continuously; crewmate is model-free but has one body and no automatic escalation.
- **Late-game tools for characters**: a deterministic DISTRIBUTION hint in `diagnose` (no other project computes a cause in Lua), copy/paste of existing builds, upgrade and deconstruct planners, logistic network views, configure and equip. claude-for-factorio has many of these but no character.
- **Published cost per unit of progress from staged mid-game saves**, and **mixed backends in one crew** (Opus foreman on the Agent SDK with DeepSeek workers over an OpenAI-compatible gateway, per `crew-friends.toml`).

Things Claude Crew lacks that others have: Space Age platforms and interplanetary travel (Codex, llm-play-factorio), any rocket-launch evidence, fog of war, human-activity yielding (Alina), an achievement regression test (factorio-partner), reply compression and idempotent job submission, and test depth. Claude Crew has a 40-check smoke test and seven test files, against Codex's ~116 test files and ai-crew's ~40 scenarios.

## Tonight is prompts and pre-flight, not mod surgery

Two constraints shape tonight. First, **Claude Crew has no platform operations** (no `platform` reference in `mod/` or `crew/`), so the crew can only help the friend's first space platform from Nauvis: produce and load what the platform needs, and diagnose ground-side bottlenecks. Second, any Lua change must be shipped to the friend's client before they connect. Project memory records that changing mod files while a client is connected breaks rejoin. So tonight's list favours prompt text, config and Python.

## Licenses, versions, names and achievements set the boundaries

**Licenses.** Code can be copied with its copyright notice from factorio-mcp (both the "factorio-mcp contributors" and Matteo Mekhail notices; [LICENSE](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/LICENSE)), factorio-codex, ai-crew, factorio-partner (which itself vendors MIT Mining Patch Planner and a belt router), AI Companion, claude-for-factorio, AI Agent Bridge, Coworker and FLE, all MIT. The hazards are factorio-mcp's `scripts/route/` (self-declared GPL-3.0-or-later because it derives from Factorio-SAT's designs), its `calc/` sidecar (AGPL), and chengjiang's MCP mod (GPL-3.0) ([LPF README](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/README.md); [calc README](https://github.com/StevenLi-phoenix/llm-play-factorio/blob/main/factorio-mcp/calc/README.md); [chengjiang portal](https://mods.factorio.com/api/mods/factorio-mcp-server/full)). Handing the mod zip to a friend so they can join is arguably distribution, so GPL code inside `claude-crew` would force GPL-compatible terms on the whole mod. Reimplement the router from its published description instead. Ideas only, no code, from: llm-play-factorio's root (`lpf/`, `scripts/`, docs), crewmate, factorioctl (its README says MIT but there is no LICENSE file), the AI Player v3 repo (the portal says MIT, the repo has no file; ask before copying), the ChatGPT fork, Schwalbe262, phiresky and jerome3o. Alina is all rights reserved and forbids derivative works ([LICENSE](https://github.com/KORTY-DEV/alina-ai-teammate/blob/99e72d4/LICENSE)).

**Factorio 2.1 vs 2.0.77.** The official release API shows **stable 2.0.77** and **experimental 2.1.22** as of today ([factorio.com/api/latest-releases](https://factorio.com/api/latest-releases)). Mods declaring `factorio_version: "2.1"`, including Toby (2.1.20+), Alina, Chat AI Player, Claude Companion and chengjiang's MCP server, will not load on Claude Crew's server, so they are irrelevant tonight however good they are. 2.1 has sat on experimental for about four months, and Wube reportedly calls it the last major update, with no firm stable date (via search summaries of [FFF #440](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-440), not read directly). When it goes stable, Claude Crew needs an `info.json` bump and an API audit. AI Companion's 2.1 port shipped as "assorted 2.x API fixes" ([PR #2](https://github.com/lveillard/factorio-ai-companion/pull/2)), and [factorio-docs-mcp](https://github.com/hjfmailbox/factorio-docs-mcp) offers cross-version API diffs, though it is unproven. Codex's and factorio-mcp's 2.0.77 targeting is part of why they are the right quarries now. Everyone joining tonight must also be on 2.0.77, not experimental.

**Mod name.** Keep `claude-crew`; don't rename to `ai-crew`. watdafawx's active MIT mod already uses the internal name `ai-crew` and the title "AI Crew" ([info.json](https://github.com/watdafawx/ai-crew/blob/main/info.json)). Factorio mods are identified by that internal name, so taking it would collide for anyone who has both installed and would invite confusion with a mod built on the opposite philosophy. Neither name is registered on the portal, and no portal mod has "crew" in its name or title ([portal API](https://mods.factorio.com/api/mods/ai-crew); [claude-crew](https://mods.factorio.com/api/mods/claude-crew)). If Claude Crew is ever published, registering `claude-crew` early reserves it. Claude Crew's remote interface is named `claude-crew` (`mod/claude-crew/control.lua` L343), so it does not collide with the `ai_player` interface that AI Player v3, Coworker and pi-factorio share. One consideration this research did not cover: the crew now runs DeepSeek workers, and "Claude" in a public mod name is a brand question. Other portal mods already use the word (Claude Companion), but whether that is acceptable for a published, model-agnostic mod was not researched.

**Achievements and `eval`.** The wiki says script or cheat console commands permanently disable a save's achievements, and any installed mod moves achievements to a separate instance that doesn't register with Steam ([wiki: Achievements](https://wiki.factorio.com/Achievements); [wiki: Console](https://wiki.factorio.com/Console)). factorio-partner tested that mod-registered commands do not trip the flag, even over RCON and even when they change the world ([ENGINE-NOTES.md](https://github.com/Fatoom333/factorio-partner/blob/main/docs/ENGINE-NOTES.md)). Claude Crew's transport is the mod command `/crew <json>`, so it should be safe by the same logic. The `eval` op runs `load()` inside that handler and refuses player callers (`mod/claude-crew/control.lua` L306–307). It likely keeps achievements too, but that is inferred and untested. The practical exposures are `scripts/rc.py --raw`, which can send `/c`, and the fact that `eval` is a legit-rules backdoor. Copy factorio-partner's end-of-test check (one `/c` on a copy save must still show the first-use warning), gate `eval` behind a dev setting on human saves, and label any benchmark run that used it as "assisted", the way Codex and conveyer do ([conveyer RULES.md](https://github.com/nulljosh/conveyer/blob/main/docs/RULES.md)). By contrast, Codex's `/silent-command remote.call` and llm-play-factorio's `/silent-command` helper scripts do disable achievements on their saves.

## Conclusion

The prior art changes what Claude Crew should be proud of and what it should stop writing itself. The coordinator → scheduler → deterministic controller split is now independently confirmed by llm-play-factorio's ~30 aborted jobs, Codex's single-writer ledger, crewmate's model-free directives, and Prime Agent's call for "least-privilege action interfaces, independent state validation, and auditable rollback" after its agent wiped its own world and learned to spawn resources over RCON ([Prime Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23552)). So the remaining open question is not architecture. It is whether the order system actually works, and nobody has tested that yet, including Claude Crew. Meanwhile the player-character layer, the part Claude Crew wrote fastest, is where others have spent the most debugging, and it is the one place where porting MIT code beats writing more.

The strategic opening is narrower and more durable than "LLMs play Factorio". The field's most-installed helpers are scripted drones that build your ghosts, and the most-requested AI behaviour is a teammate that divides work, stays out of your designs and can be told what to do. No legit, multi-character, multiplayer project exists to meet that demand. If tonight's session shows two humans steering a crew with a sentence like "go fix our iron bottleneck", and the cost and replay are published, Claude Crew holds a position that the projects with better game layers have each ruled out by design.
